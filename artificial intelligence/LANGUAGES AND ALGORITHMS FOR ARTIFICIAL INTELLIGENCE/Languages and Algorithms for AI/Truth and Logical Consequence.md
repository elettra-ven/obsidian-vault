The absolute notion of "truth" given by the sensible world must be replaced by the concept of **logical consequence**.

The truth changes by interpreting the words used in a sentence in different ways (for example, spherical geometry $vs$ Euclidean geometry).

>[!success] Logical Consequence
>>[!quote] Sentence $F_i$
>>An axiom that is True in a certain set of worlds.
>
>
>Let $\Gamma = F_1,...,F_n$ be a set of sentences (or a **theory**) and $F$ be a **conclusion**.
>
>$F$ is a **logical consequence of $\Gamma$** ($F\models\Gamma$) if, for different interpretations of the formulas in the various possible worlds, it is always true that:
>
>if all the formulas in $\Gamma$ are true, within the world in examination, then also $F$ is true.
>

The more hypothesis we put in the theory, the smaller the intersection, and the bigger the number of logical consequences.

>[!done] Logical Equivalence
>$F$ is ==logically equivalent== to $G$ if $F$ is a logical consequence of $G$ and $G$ is a logical consequence of $F$.
>$$F \equiv G \text{ iff } F\models G \text{ and } G\models F $$
>This is called **Logical Equivalence**.

>[!info] A theory is interesting if it allows some models (if it's not an empty intersection of axioms).

$\implies$ Truth is always defined with respect to a world and a give interpretation of its symbols.

>[!danger] It's not the truth of a single sentence that is interesting, it's the notion of logical consequence and equivalence (that doesn't change with the world)
